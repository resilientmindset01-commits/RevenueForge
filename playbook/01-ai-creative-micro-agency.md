## The model in one line
Make one finished sample for a real business before anyone pays, turn it into a portfolio, then offer the same
package to businesses that already spend money on ads or listings.

## The method
1. PICK BUYERS WHO ALREADY PAY FOR ATTENTION. His sources, best-converting first in his runs:
   - Brands whose ads have run for a long time in the Meta Ad Library (a long-running ad is usually paying back).
     Ads with a direct WhatsApp link (search the ad library for the wa.me link) replied fastest; his first and
     largest sale came this way.
   - Etsy and Facebook Marketplace sellers who have already made sales (they have stock and already spend).
   - Real estate listings (Zillow, Redfin, Airbnb): agents handle many properties, so one client repeats.
   - Local businesses with good reviews on Google Maps: the lowest yield in his numbers.
2. BUILD ONE SAMPLE PACKAGE FIRST. Stack several deliverables so the package reads as premium: a cinematic 9:16
   walkthrough, a website, a product ad, 3-5 static ad creatives, or 3 UGC-style ads with different angles.
   The Claude side, checked in the Claude Help Center on 2026-09-15: an image or video tool that offers a remote MCP
   server is added at Customize > Connectors > "+" > "Add custom connector" (Free plans are limited to one custom
   connector), and a reusable prompt procedure is uploaded as a skill ZIP at Customize > Skills (needs code execution
   enabled). Anthropic's warning applies: custom connectors reach "arbitrary services that have not been verified by
   Anthropic", so connect only vendors you trust and hand them only the files the job needs.
3. BRAND AND PORTFOLIO. One name, one logo, one portfolio page showing every deliverable, one dedicated email address.
   He hosts on Vercel with a cheap custom domain; treat his "$0.99 .online" as a first-year promotion and check the
   renewal price before buying.
4. PERSONALIZED OUTREACH. Name the business, say what it sells, point at one specific improvement, link the portfolio.
   Keep prices off the portfolio and share them after interest; anchor with itemized prices, then a bundle discount
   (his: $745 itemized, $149 as a bundle).
5. GET PAID SAFELY. Send watermarked work before payment only to a business you have verified, or take 50% upfront;
   release clean files after payment.
6. MAKE IT RECURRING. New products, new listings and ad fatigue create repeat orders; offer a monthly package (his:
   $810 a month for a brand that wanted daily videos).

7. PUBLISH ONE SAMPLE INSTEAD OF SENDING IT. Pick a named business in the niche that already spends on attention and
   post the rebuild in public: what their current output is made of, the one diagnosis, the positioning sentence,
   four pillars, and the weekly cadence that produces it from material they already record. Name the prospect. The
   post reaches them, and every similar buyer reads it as a work sample. Do not claim a result you have not produced
   for them, and do not present their assets as your own work. (@wizofecom, 11 X Articles read 2026-09-18; every figure of his is his own claim and is not carried.)
8. KEEP A FOLLOW-UP LEDGER. One row per conversation: what they want, the objection they raised, the constraint they
   named, the date. Every later contact references a specific line from that row rather than being a check-in. Closes
   in this lane arrive after several touches spread over months, so the ledger is the asset and the pitch is not.

## His numbers, as reported (UNVERIFIED, tiny samples)
- Local-business run: 100 personalized emails, "a couple" of interested replies, 1 sale at $149. His description:
  "gross revenue from one client during this test".
- 7-day run: cold email 0 sales; Instagram posts 1 sale ($57); direct WhatsApp outreach to advertisers 2 clients,
  one on an $810 monthly package. $987 revenue, $572.20 claimed profit after his own 40% production-cost estimate.
One to three clients per experiment. The method can produce a first sale; these are not rates to plan on.

## Risk flags -- handle these or do not run it
- COLD EMAIL IS REGULATED, B2B INCLUDED. The FTC's CAN-SPAM compliance guide: "The law makes no exception for
  business-to-business email." Commercial email needs honest headers and subject lines, identification as an ad, your
  physical postal address and a working opt-out, with opt-outs honored "within 10 business days"; each violating email
  can cost "up to $53,088". The drafts shown in the video carry no address or opt-out line. This applies when mailing
  US businesses.
- PHOTOS YOU DO NOT OWN. Google Maps, Etsy and listing photos belong to someone else. His own description says "Always
  obtain permission before using business photos or branding". Build samples from your own photos, stock footage, or a
  business that agreed, and keep luxury brands out of the portfolio (one of his samples used a Chanel bag listing).
- AI WORK THAT LOOKS REAL. Label realistic AI-generated video where the platform requires it (TikTok asks creators to
  label realistic AI-generated content; confirm the current rule before posting), and tell clients which assets are AI
  concepts rather than their real premises.
- WAY 4, AN AI FASHION INFLUENCER EARNING AFFILIATE COMMISSIONS -> NOT A PLAY. The FTC expects a financial relationship
  with a brand to be disclosed clearly, platforms expect realistic AI content to be labelled, and an AI persona selling
  clothes on commission is a brand risk even when both are disclosed.

## FIT for the operator
STANDALONE: a tool subscription plus a domain to start; days to weeks to a first dollar; weak proof (self-reported,
one to three sales). PORTFOLIO: EARNING-MAP avenue 6 (AI-powered service) in its lowest-ticket form, built on what the
forges already teach -- prompts packaged as skills, tools attached as connectors. The version worth testing: businesses
in the operator's own city, approached warm or in person, with photos taken with permission. That removes the two
largest risk flags at once. Launch is held: no outreach or spend until the operator says go.
