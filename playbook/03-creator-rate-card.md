# Creator and freelance rate card -- what things actually pay

Assembled 2026-09-16 from 149 Silicon Valley Girl transcripts (2018-2026). Every figure is the speaker's own
self-reported number, most from her own books or from people she hired, so treat them as a range to negotiate
against rather than a market survey. Dates matter: rates from 2019-2021 are marked, since freelance and ad pricing
has moved since.

## Platform economics she has published
- AdSense by topic and audience beats audience size. In 2020 her business channel (~600k subscribers, US-heavy) earned
  $39,479 at a $13.76 CPM, while her language channel (~3.1M subscribers) earned $79,965 at roughly a third of that
  CPM. In 2019 the same pattern: CPM ~$10 business, ~$5 language, ~$2.70 Russian-language.
- 2021 across three channels: $240,311 of AdSense, which paid a team of seven.
- Shorts pay in subscribers, not money: a 1M-view short returned $53 over 19 days and 22,000 subscribers.
- Creator share of ad revenue is 55% on long-form.
- Paid follower growth: $17,000 of ads and influencer stories bought about 50,000 Instagram followers, roughly $0.33
  per follower, and she says the engagement did not follow the count.
- Email: 21-27% of company sales came from email; a 46,000-person list produced $40K and $30K webinar launches.

## A full product P&L, the rarest thing here
A branded planner: $25,000 of revenue from 500 units over about three months, against a copywriter at $2,000, a
designer at $2,000, printing at $9,000 and about 4% in fees, so roughly $7,000 of profit. Her own verdict was that it
was not worth it. The upsell ladder she planned around it was $49 notebook, $150 course, $2,000 trip.

## How to use this
1. Price your own offer against the sell-side table, then check what the same output costs to buy on the buy side.
   Several of her services resell work she buys for a tenth of the price; that gap is the business.
2. Treat any single number as one data point from one seller. Where two sources in the table disagree, prefer the
   later year.
3. Nothing here proves demand. It tells you what a price looks like when someone is already selling.
