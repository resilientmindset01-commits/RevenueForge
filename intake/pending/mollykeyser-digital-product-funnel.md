## The funnel (one line)
YouTube (free traffic) -> FREEBIE (free lead magnet, given for name+email) -> EMAIL LIST ->
automated emails with CTAs -> CHECKOUT PAGE -> digital product. Repeat weekly.

## The four pieces (her "channel / freebie / product / checkout")
1. ONE channel, ONE niche. Don't split energy across channels/topics. Pick one focus (for
   the operator: AI coding / Claude Code -- a DEV audience, kept separate from PrepBrix/NEET).
   Sub-topics live under it (skills, CLAUDE.md, context, agents).
2. FREEBIE = a free, DONE-FOR-YOU digital thing (template/checklist/PDF/script) delivered for
   name+email. Its only job: convert YouTube VIEWERS -> EMAIL subscribers. Done-for-you beats
   a long ebook (higher download/conversion). Mention it 2-3x per video (a clear CTA).
3. PRODUCT = solve a PAINFUL problem the audience actually has. Don't build what's fun for
   you; ask them (survey/poll once you have ~100-200 subs). Painful problem = faster sales.
4. CHECKOUT PAGE = auto-delivers the product on purchase (Gumroad already does this).

## The mechanics that matter
- YouTube PAYS you to make content (AdSense at 1,000 subs + 4,000 watch hours), unlike paid
  ads which cost money + endless new creative. She claims ~$5k/mo AdSense (UNVERIFIED).
- SUBSCRIBERS don't matter; VIEWS do. The algorithm ranks on click-through-rate + average-
  view-duration, not subs. You can make sales from day 1 with <100 views/video by converting
  a slice of viewers -> email -> sale. (Students getting 300 views + 20 subs already selling.)
- EMAIL LIST is the asset you OWN (YouTube is rented). "Email is ~40x more effective at sales
  than social alone" -- commonly cited, treat as directional. Automate a welcome sequence
  (day 1 / 5 / 10...) written ONCE, each email ending in a CTA to the checkout.
- Model what works: study successful channels in your niche -- what freebies + products do
  they sell? Copy the business-model shape, not the content.
- Consistency: 1-2 videos/week, improve each one, commit ~3 months. "Take action, confidence
  follows." This is the real cost -- time, not money.
